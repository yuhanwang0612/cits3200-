1. Data Sources & Joins

Each record in the database is the result of joining three independent sources:

Researcher profiles

Staff lists are scraped directly from each university's research portal. For universities with structured staff exports (Monash, Adelaide, UQ, ANU), a *_staff.csv is produced containing name, job title, academic level, field of research, profile URL, and ORCID where available. For universities without a structured export (UniMelb, USyd, UWA, UNSW), researcher records are synthesised from the author fields in their publication data.

Publications

Publication metadata — title, DOI, year, venue, citation counts — is fetched from the OpenAlex API using each researcher's name and ORCID as identifiers. A publication is linked to a researcher when at least one author's ORCID or display name matches. Co-authored papers are counted once per matching author, so a paper with two staff co-authors appears twice in the database.

Journal rankings

Journal quality data comes from three sources, joined to publications by ISSN (preferred) then journal name:

  • ABDC Journal Quality List 2025 — sourced from anu_journals.csv. Provides quality_rank (A*, A, B, C) and abdc_edition.
  • Scimago Journal Rankings — sourced from scimago.csv. Provides sjr, sjr_quartile, and cites_per_doc_2y.
  • Clarivate Journal Impact Factor (JCR) — sourced from jcr.csv (fetched via the Web of Science Journals API). Provides impact_factor and jcr_year. Only available for journals with a current JCR subscription.

2. Standardisation Pipeline

Step 1 — Scrape

Each university has a dedicated scraper (*_scraper.py) that writes *_staff.csv and *_publications.csv to the project root.

Step 2 — Merge

merge_publications.py concatenates all eight *_publications.csv files into combined_publications.csv, normalising university names and column headers to a shared schema.

Step 3 — Load

load.py reads combined_publications.csv, anu_journals.csv, and jcr.csv (if present), performs the ISSN-first journal join, and writes everything into the SQLite database (research.db) via SQLAlchemy.

ISSN-first matching

Journal records are matched by ISSN whenever an ISSN is present in both the publication and the journal list. If no ISSN match is found, the pipeline falls back to a normalised journal-name comparison. This two-step approach handles journals that appear under slightly different names across sources.

OpenAlex percentile

Where available, OpenAlex returns a cited_by_percentile_year.min value indicating a publication's citation standing within its year and field. This is stored as citation_percentile in the database.

4. Ranking Metrics

ABDC Journal Quality List

The Australian Business Deans Council (ABDC) Journal Quality List rates journals in business and economics. The 2025 edition is used. The quality_rank field takes one of five values:

A*Top 7% of journals in the field. Highest quality.
AHigh quality, well regarded by the field.
BRecognised quality journals in the field.
CRecognised journals, lower tier.
"none"Journal was checked against the ABDC list but received no rating. Distinct from null (not yet checked).

Scimago Journal Rankings (SJR)

Scimago provides three metrics sourced from Elsevier Scopus data:

  • sjr — weighted citation score; accounts for the prestige of the citing journals.
  • sjr_quartile — Q1 (top 25%) to Q4 (bottom 25%) within the journal's subject category.
  • cites_per_doc_2y — average citations per document over the preceding two years (analogous to a 2-year impact factor).

Clarivate Journal Impact Factor (JCR)

impact_factor is the Clarivate 2-year Journal Impact Factor from the Web of Science Journal Citation Reports. It is available only for journals indexed in Web of Science; null means the journal was not found or is not covered by the subscription.

OpenAlex Citation Percentile

citation_percentile is OpenAlex's field- and year-normalised citation percentile (0–100) for the individual publication, not the journal. A value of 95 means the paper is cited more than 95% of papers in the same field published in the same year.

5. Known Limitations

Co-authorship counting. Publication counts are researcher–publication pairs. A paper co-authored by two staff members at the same university appears twice in the database — once for each author. Totals shown on the site reflect this.
Repository-only coverage. Publications are identified from each university's research repository. Papers not deposited in the repository — including some older works or works under embargo — will not appear even if the researcher is otherwise present.
Name-based matching. Researchers without an ORCID iD are matched by display name. Common names, name variants, and transliterations may produce false matches or missed matches.
Journal coverage gaps. Not every journal has an ABDC rating, a Scimago entry, or a JCR Impact Factor. Null values in those columns indicate the journal was not found in the respective list, not that the journal is unranked.
UniMelb, USyd, UWA, UNSW researcher metadata. These four universities do not expose a structured staff export. Researcher rows are synthesised from the author fields of publication records, so job title and profile URL are not available for most of their staff.

6. Worked Example

A researcher at Monash University publishes a paper in the Journal of Finance (ISSN 0022-1082). Here is how the record is assembled:

  1. Scraper finds the researcher on the Monash research portal and writes a row to monash_staff.csv and a publication row to monash_publications.csv with ISSN 0022-1082.
  2. merge_publications.py copies the row into combined_publications.csv with university = "Monash University".
  3. load.py looks up 0022-1082 in anu_journals.csv and finds quality_rank = "A*". It then looks up the same ISSN in scimago.csv and attaches sjr, sjr_quartile, and cites_per_doc_2y. Finally it checks jcr.csv and attaches impact_factor if present.
  4. OpenAlex returns citation metadata and a field-year percentile, which is stored as citation_percentile on the Publication record.
  5. The front end displays the researcher's publication with an A* badge and the numerical metrics in the table columns.
